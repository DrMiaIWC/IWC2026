What is Neurotechnology?

Neurotechnology is technology that interacts with the brain and nervous system to help us understand, repair, and enhance human capabilities.

Why It Matters

🧠 Medical Advances

Helping people recover movement and communication abilities.

💡 Brain Research

Giving scientists new ways to understand how the brain works.

🤖 Human-Computer Interaction

Allowing devices to be controlled using brain signals.

The Future of Neurotechnology

Future neurotechnology could help treat neurological disorders, improve prosthetic limbs, and create new ways for humans to communicate with technology.

Our Mission

At Neuro Nerds, our mission is to educate people about neurotechnology and its growing impact on society. Through interactive learning, engaging visuals, and accessible information, we aim to inspire curiosity about the future of brain science and technology.

History of Neurotechnology & Innovators

The history of neurotechnology is shaped not only by scientific breakthroughs, but also by the researchers and innovators whose work continues to expand our understanding of the brain. This timeline highlights both major developments and the contributions of innovators helping shape the future of the field.

1924

German scientist Hans Berger recorded the first human EEG, allowing researchers to measure brain activity.

1960s

Scientists began experimenting with electrical stimulation of the brain, laying the foundation for modern neurotechnology.

1990s

Early Brain-Computer Interfaces (BCIs) enabled direct communication between the brain and computers.

2000s

⭐ Dr. Sheila Nirenberg

Dr. Nirenberg's work on neural coding and artificial vision systems helped advance technologies designed to restore sight.

2010s

Neuroprosthetics and neural implants became increasingly advanced, helping individuals regain movement and communication.

Present Day

⭐ Black Women in Neuroscience

Researchers such as Dr. M. Danielle Graham and many other Black women continue contributing to neuroscience, brain research, and the future of neurotechnology.

Future

Neurotechnology may enable more advanced BCIs, improved treatments for neurological disorders, and new ways for humans to interact with technology.
